## A. Executive Summary

The Play purchase path is implemented in source: Android Buy Now routes to `PlayBilling`, creates a server order, queries a one-time Play product, submits a purchase token to Supabase, settles a purchase and entitlement atomically, and retries consumption and refund reconciliation. The production and internal-testing EAS profiles select `play`; the separate Razorpay APK profile selects `razorpay`. The intended application ID is **`in.medveda.app`** and is already registered, per the supplied business decision ([app.json](mobile-app/app.json), lines 1-20; [eas.json](mobile-app/eas.json), lines 42-72).

The checked-in tier seed is **empty** ([play_price_tiers.json](supabase/seed/play_price_tiers.json), lines 1-4). No active hosted tiers, approved product prices, Console product IDs/options, merged release manifest, final AAB, resolved BillingClient version, deployed Supabase functions/cron, or licensed purchase is evidenced. Earlier reports that describe a current `notes_1` seed are stale; the hosted database could still have such a row and was not queried. The safe outcome is **NO-GO**, with **0 CRITICAL, 5 HIGH, 4 MEDIUM, 1 LOW** findings below.

## B. Current Google Play Billing Architecture

`StoreScreen.handleBuy` and other Notes controls call `usePurchase.buy` ([StoreScreen.tsx](mobile-app/src/screens/store/StoreScreen.tsx), lines 101-104; [UnlockOptionsPanel.tsx](mobile-app/src/components/UnlockOptionsPanel.tsx), lines 44-52). The hook routes Play to `PlayBilling` ([usePurchase.ts](mobile-app/src/hooks/usePurchase.ts), lines 25-40). The backend consists of `create-play-order`, `verify-play-purchase`, `_shared/googlePlay.ts`, `settle_play_purchase`, `play-payment-worker`, Play job/cursor SQL, and `reverse_play_payment` ([create-play-order/index.ts](supabase/functions/create-play-order/index.ts), lines 5-27; [verify-play-purchase/index.ts](supabase/functions/verify-play-purchase/index.ts), lines 7-68; [googlePlay.ts](supabase/functions/_shared/googlePlay.ts), lines 23-96; [google_play_ucb.sql](supabase/migrations/20261012100000_google_play_ucb.sql), lines 123-145 and 206-294; [play-payment-worker/index.ts](supabase/functions/play-payment-worker/index.ts), lines 25-308). `orders`, `payments`, `purchases`, `play_price_tiers`, `play_payment_jobs`, `play_void_tokens`, and `play_recovery_cursor` are the relevant database objects. All hosted state is **NOT VERIFIED**.

## F. Product Query and Purchase Options

`fetchMatchingPlayProduct` requests one `type: "in-app"` SKU, finds the matching Android product, requires `currency === "INR"`, compares its numeric price with the server amount, and delegates offer choice ([playBillingService.ts](mobile-app/src/services/playBillingService.ts), lines 284-295). `selectStandardPlayOffer` requires exactly one full-price INR option with an offer token and excludes discount, rent and preorder metadata ([playOfferSelection.ts](mobile-app/src/services/playOfferSelection.ts), lines 1-27). `requestPurchase` passes that token with the SKU and obfuscated IDs ([playBillingService.ts](mobile-app/src/services/playBillingService.ts), lines 253-267); installed JS forwards `offerToken` to native ([index.js](mobile-app/node_modules/expo-iap/build/index.js), lines 384-411); installed Kotlin forwards it to OpenIAP ([ExpoIapModule.kt](mobile-app/node_modules/expo-iap/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/iap/ExpoIapModule.kt), lines 291-305). This is **PASS at wrapper/source level**, **NOT VERIFIED on a Play device**.

Google's [one-time purchase option documentation](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/one-time-product-multi-purchase-options-offers) requires product details and the selected offer token for multiple-option products. The current selector deliberately supports one standard Buy option only; a product with additional eligible offers will fail closed. Missing products, wrong currency/price, or missing option cause a visible error and no sheet. The Store shows catalog prices (for example [UnlockOptionsPanel.tsx](mobile-app/src/components/UnlockOptionsPanel.tsx), line 92); actual localized Play price is checked at purchase time but not displayed on the billing screen. Product availability, older Play Store behavior, already-owned responses and the exact native ProductDetails/offer mapping need licensed-device testing. The old `purchases.products.get` response does not independently prove the configured Console price; client comparison is a guard, not server-side Console price attestation.

A later order-snapshot trigger sets the entitlement scope from the original order ([play_entitlement_order_snapshot.sql](supabase/migrations/20261015120000_play_entitlement_order_snapshot.sql), lines 6-30). The function/trigger has local SQL test evidence, but migration deployment is **NOT VERIFIED**.

No client price or success flag is accepted as proof of payment. Invalid purchases fail closed. Server consumption happens only after durable settlement. Hosted function deployment, Google API permissions, service account secret, RLS, data integrity and actual Google response behavior are **NOT VERIFIED**.

## J. Purchase Consumption and Recovery

The direct verification function consumes after settlement and records `consumed` or `failed`; a paid order remains if consumption fails ([verify-play-purchase/index.ts](supabase/functions/verify-play-purchase/index.ts), lines 41-59). The Play-only worker authenticates a cron secret, enqueues paid orders with pending/failed consumption, claims leased jobs, checks Google again, consumes, retries 409 races, and updates the order ([play-payment-worker/index.ts](supabase/functions/play-payment-worker/index.ts), lines 25-47, 59-166 and 242-308; [play_payment_jobs.sql](supabase/migrations/20261015100000_play_payment_jobs.sql), lines 49-110). Failed jobs back off and park after 12 attempts; an alert is emitted (lines 92-110; worker lines 160-166). The cron file is explicitly **review-only** and would schedule consume/reconcile/void jobs if executed ([play_payment_cron_setup.sql](supabase/play_payment_cron_setup.sql), lines 1-4 and 23-53). Deployment, scheduling, monitor delivery, acknowledgement timing and recovery from a parked job are **NOT VERIFIED**. Google documents granting access then consuming a consumable purchase in its [one-time purchase lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/one-time).

## K. Refund and Restore Handling

The worker reconciles paid Play orders with Google, sweeps voided purchases through a cursor, retains void tokens, and invokes `reverse_play_payment` ([play-payment-worker/index.ts](supabase/functions/play-payment-worker/index.ts), lines 69-139 and 169-239; [play_payment_jobs.sql](supabase/migrations/20261015100000_play_payment_jobs.sql), lines 27-45 and 112-169). The reversal SQL changes paid orders and success purchases to refunded and handles referral credits with idempotent status transitions ([google_play_refunds.sql](supabase/migrations/20261012110000_google_play_refunds.sql), lines 21-37 and 108-115). Access checks require `purchases.status='success'`, so a reversed entitlement should stop authorizing Notes ([free_preview.sql](supabase/migrations/20260924150000_free_preview.sql), lines 72-84). On-device restore is described in H. No RTDN receiver is implemented; the source uses scheduled polling. Real refund, void timing, account switch, reinstall, duplicate refund, worker outage and recovery require hosted/device validation. Google recommends backend lifecycle handling and documents pending-to-purchased transitions in its [lifecycle guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/one-time).
